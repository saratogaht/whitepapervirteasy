Executive summary

Simulation as an added practice layer, not a replacement

Across 18 studies and 3 reviews, virtual haptic simulation works best before the phantom head and the patient: students repeat procedures at any hour, with objective data on every attempt.

Learning pathway

Randomized and comparative studies in implantology, endodontics and restorative dentistry report faster or better early skill, and lower stress and anxiety, when virtual simulator practice comes first.

Productivity

Objective scoring is documented. Gains in instructor time, consumables and lab scheduling are plausible but not yet measured in controlled studies, and a pilot can measure them.

Honest limits

Not every result is favorable. A headset lowered performance in one study, and productivity gains are still hypotheses. The paper says so plainly.

The learning pathway

Where simulation fits between theory and the clinic

The research places the simulator in the middle of the path, between knowledge and supervised patient care.

1

Orientation

Anatomy, instruments and ergonomics on screen.

2

Fundamentals

Repeated drills in implant, cavity and access preparation.

3

Feedback

Objective scores and expert review of each attempt.

4

Transfer

Phantom-head work, with a planned transition period.

5

Clinic

Supervised patient care once the skill is shown.

Design principle: move students on by demonstrated competence, not hours logged.

For deans and administrators

Four decisions you can take away

  • Treat the virtual simulator as an added practice step with entry and exit criteria for each skill, not as a substitute for phantom hours.
  • Prefer the headset-free set-up for complex tasks such as access cavity preparation.
  • Budget for faculty calibration and for a transition period back to conventional simulators.
  • Measure before you scale: run a 90-day pilot with KPIs for instructor hours, lab occupancy, consumables and time to competence.

Why you can trust it

This is not a brochure. The paper reports what the evidence supports and what it does not.

  • Unfavorable results are included, not filtered out.
  • Gaps are named: productivity gains and long-term outcomes are not yet measured in controlled studies.
  • Every claim is tied to a published source, listed in the references.
  • Installation figures come from the Virteasy installation list supplied for the paper (2015–2026).
